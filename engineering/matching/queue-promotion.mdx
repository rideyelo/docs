A driver's queue is every ride with `driver_id = driver` and `status = 'queued'`. Rides enter it through a manual accept while the driver is busy, or through auto-queue. They leave it through promotion to `driver_en_route`, cancellation, requeue, or offline unqueue.

For the product view, see [Matching and auto-queue](/concepts/matching-and-auto-queue#queue-promotion-and-recovery).

## Queue order

Every queue query uses the same order:

```sql
ORDER BY COALESCE(match_time, request_time), id
```

`match_time` is set to `now()` by `AssignAcceptedRideToDriver`. The queue is first-assigned, first-served. It isn't ordered by confirm time, pickup distance, or fare. `request_time` only matters for rows with no `match_time`, which the assignment path never produces.

## How rides enter the queue

`AssignAcceptedRideToDriver` in `queries/ride_lifecycle.sql` decides the status:

```sql
status = CASE WHEN NOT sqlc.arg(auto_queued)::boolean AND sqlc.arg(queue_length)::bigint = 0
    THEN 'driver_en_route' ELSE 'queued' END,
```

Its caller is `reserveAcceptanceAssignment` in `internal/data/repository/ride_acceptance_operation.go`, the reservation step of an `accept` ride operation. It never sets the `auto_queued` argument, so the status depends on `queue_length` alone. For an automatic assignment, `MarkRideAutomaticallyAssigned` sets `Rides.auto_queued = true` in a separate statement afterwards.

`queue_length` comes from `GetRideAcceptingDriver` and counts every ride assigned to the driver that isn't `complete` or `pending_rider_rating`. That includes the active ride.

| Path | Driver has no rides | Driver has rides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual accept | `driver_en_route` | `queued` |
| Auto-queue | `driver_en_route` | `queued` |

An acceptance into the active slot authorizes payment and computes the pickup route inside the same `accept` operation. An acceptance into the queue does neither. Its `Queued` flag is frozen in the operation's inputs, the operation finishes without provider work, and the later `promotion` reuses the inputs frozen at acceptance. See [Accept and promotion](/engineering/payments/ride-operation-journal#accept-and-promotion).

## The two promotion paths

There are two SQL statements that move a queued ride to `driver_en_route`. They have different guards.

| | `PromoteNextQueuedRide` | `PromoteAutoQueuedRide` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Called from | The finalizers of `complete`, `cancel` (terminal or requeue), and a declined authorization, inside their transactions | `ReserveIdle`, the reservation of an idle promotion |
| Picks | Queue head for the driver | The given ride, only if it's the queue head |
| No active ride | Yes | Yes |
| Driver online | — | `is_online_driver` |
| Recent ping | — | `last_pinged_driver` within 5 minutes (`OfflineGraceInterval`) |
| Session | — | Exactly one open session, matching the ride's vehicle and fleet |

Both paths end the same way. In the transaction that flips the status, `reserveRidePromotion` inserts a `promotion` ride operation for the promoted ride, with its `ride_operation` job. The ID is `uuid.NewV5` of `yelo:ride:promotion:{rideID}:{assignmentGeneration}`, so the two paths can't both promote one assignment.

### The promotion operation

The promoted ride is `driver_en_route` as soon as that transaction commits. Its payment and route follow in the `promotion` operation, and the ride reports `transition_pending` until the operation finishes.

1. `RidePreparationPaymentExecutor.Prepare` authorizes the ride. A free ride needs nothing. A ride that already has a `payment_intent_id` is inspected read-only and never re-authorized. Otherwise it clones the payment method, creates the intent, and confirms it.
2. `RideService.PreparePickupRoute` computes the pickup route.
3. `RidePreparationCommands.Finalize` attaches the payment columns and the route, runs the revision finalizer, inserts the `queue_promoted` ride event, enqueues the rider's `ride_confirmed` push, and finishes the operation. All of that is one transaction.
4. The operation's job refreshes the driver's queue summary. See [Terminal effects](/engineering/payments/ride-operation-journal#terminal-effects).

After `complete`, `cancel`, or a declined authorization, nothing in the request runs these steps. The `ride_operation` job for the new `promotion` does. A Stripe or routing error leaves the operation `pending`, and the job retries. A declined card finishes the operation as `failed`: the promoted ride is canceled with `cancel_reason = 'Payment Failed'`, the rider gets `ride_payment_failed`, and the driver's next queued ride is promoted in turn.

### Idle recovery

`RideService.RecoverIdleQueue` handles a queue with no active ride. `RefreshQueueSummaries` calls it every 3 minutes for every driver with queued rides, 10 at a time.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check for an active ride">
    `GetDriverActiveRideInfo` returns `nil` error when an active ride exists, and recovery stops. Only a `NotFound` continues.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reserve the promotion">
    `PromoteIdle` calls `ReserveIdle` for the queue head. Under the ride command locks, `claimIdlePromotion` requires the ride to be `queued` for this driver at the same `assignment_generation`, then runs `PromoteAutoQueuedRide`. If that updates no row, the reservation fails with "Ride Unavailable" and nothing changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute">
    `PromoteIdle` runs the promotion operation in the same call, as described above. If it fails midway, the operation's job continues it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A second pass for the same assignment finds the existing operation by its deterministic ID and replays it. `RefreshQueueSummaries` logs "Failed to recover idle driver queue" at warn level for each driver whose recovery returns an error, including a failed guard, and then refreshes that driver's queue summary either way.

<Note>
The session-level `pg_advisory_lock` in `Ride.AcquireLock` is taken by manual accept (`acceptFinancialRide`), not by idle recovery. The key is the first 8 bytes of the ride UUID as a signed `int64`. It's released with `context.WithoutCancel`, so a cancelled request can't skip the unlock. If `pg_advisory_unlock` itself fails, the error is logged and the connection goes back to the pool still holding the session lock. The next caller that draws that connection can re-enter it, and every other connection blocks on that ride until the connection is recycled.
</Note>

### Stranded queues

`PromoteAutoQueuedRide` refuses to promote when the driver isn't online, hasn't pinged in 5 minutes, has zero or several open sessions, or has a session that doesn't match the ride's `driver_vehicle_id` and `fleet_id`.

The session guard rarely trips in practice. `RefreshDriverSession` refuses to replace a driver's session while they have any ride that isn't `complete` or `pending_rider_rating`, including `queued` ones (`refreshSessionOwnership` in `internal/data/repository/driver_availability.go`). Fleet membership edits are refused under the same condition.

The common stranded case is a stale ping. Recovery skips the driver on every 3-minute pass until [offline unqueue](#offline-unqueue) returns the ride to `confirmed`, which needs no presence and no ping for 5 minutes. The expiry sweep only touches `confirmed` rides, so a queued ride never expires on its own.

## Offline unqueue

`UserService.handleQueuedDriverOfflineGrace` runs at the end of `PurgeOfflineDrivers` on every `Operational` tick. For each driver with queued rides:

| Condition | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `presence:{driverId}` exists | Delete `driver:offline_grace:{driverId}`, keep the queue |
| Presence missing, grace key present | Wait |
| Presence missing, no grace key, last ping under 5 minutes ago | Set the grace key with a 5-minute TTL, wait |
| Presence missing, no grace key, last ping 5 minutes or more ago | `unqueueOfflineDriverRides` |

The grace key doesn't reset the clock. Its TTL is only a throttle. If the driver's last ping was 1 minute old when the key was set, unqueue happens when the key expires, 6 minutes after the last ping. Unqueue happens between 5 and 10 minutes after the last ping, plus up to one 30-second tick.

`Ride.UnqueueDriverRides` locks the driver's participants, rechecks `last_pinged_driver` under lock, and runs:

```sql
UPDATE "Rides" SET
    status = 'confirmed', driver_id = NULL, match_time = NULL,
    auto_queued = FALSE, driver_vehicle_id = NULL, fleet_id = NULL
WHERE driver_id = sqlc.arg(driver_id)::uuid AND status = 'queued'
RETURNING id, community_id;
```

The service then deletes the queue summary and ETAs and calls `rideBoard.AddRide` for each ride. It doesn't push anyone.

<Warning>
Unqueued rides lose their fleet and keep their original `confirm_time`, payment intent, and `declined_drivers`. Because `fleet_id` is null, the fleet-filtered board hides them and T0 skips them. Because `confirm_time` is unchanged, a ride confirmed more than 10 minutes ago is expired by the next `CancelUnmatchedRides` sweep. A queued ride normally has no payment intent yet. If one is still attached, the next driver's `accept` voids it before authorizing, and expiry voids it before completing the ride.
</Warning>

## Queue summaries

`QueueSummaryService` in `internal/service/activation/queue_summary.go` owns two Redis keys per driver, both with a 30-minute TTL:

| Key | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `driver:queue_summary:{driverId}` | Hash: `ride_count`, `total_fare_cents`, `total_minutes`, `total_miles`, `last_dropoff_lat`, `last_dropoff_lng`, `calculated_at` |
| `driver:queue_ride_etas:{driverId}` | JSON list of `ride_id`, `position`, `estimated_start_minutes`, `deadhead_minutes`, `ride_minutes` |

`CalculateAndCacheQueueSummary` walks the queue in order:

| Leg | Origin | Destination | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deadhead for position 1 | Active ride's dropoff, else driver's stored position | Pickup | Mapbox `GetDistance`, 0 on error or `(0, 0)` origin |
| Deadhead for position n | Previous queued ride's dropoff | Pickup | Mapbox |
| Ride leg | Pickup | Destination | Stored `Rides.distance` and `duration` |

```
EstimatedStartMinutes[n] = cumulative + deadhead[n]
cumulative += deadhead[n] + rideMinutes[n] + 2
TotalMinutes = sum(rideMinutes) + sum(deadhead) + 2 * rideCount
```

`TotalMinutes` excludes the active ride's remaining time. Estimated start times are relative to when the driver becomes free, not to now. All minute values use integer division of seconds.

### Reads validate against Postgres

`QueueSummaryService.Read` always loads the queue from Postgres first. It serves cached ETAs only if the cached ride IDs match the queue exactly, in order. Any mismatch rebuilds through `RefreshRides`. The auto-queue pass reads the hash directly without this check.

`Refresh` runs from two places. `ReconcileRideOperation` refreshes the drivers involved in every finished ride operation: accept, auto-assign, promotion, complete, cancel, and requeue. The 3-minute loop refreshes every driver with queued rides.

## Gotchas and edge cases

- **Two promotion paths, two rule sets.** A queue advanced by completing or cancelling a ride promotes without checking presence, ping freshness, or session. A queue advanced by recovery checks all three.
- **Promoted before prepared.** The rider sees `driver_en_route` before the pickup route and payment authorization exist. The `promotion` operation is durable, so a crash doesn't strand the ride, but the ride accepts no other command until the operation finishes.
- **A queued ride is authorized late.** The card is first charged a hold when the ride is promoted, which can be long after the rider confirmed. A decline at that point cancels the ride.
- **Recovery logs guard failures.** A driver who is offline, has a stale ping, or has a mismatched session produces a "Failed to recover idle driver queue" warning on every 3-minute pass while the ride stays `queued`.
- **Advisory lock collisions share a lock.** Two rides whose UUIDs share the first 8 bytes serialize against each other on manual accept. With random v4 UUIDs this is negligible.
- **The 3-minute loop does N Mapbox calls per driver.** `RefreshQueueSummaries` rebuilds every summary with one `GetDistance` per queued ride, whether or not anything changed.
- **Auto-queue reads unvalidated summaries.** A summary is refreshed by the operation's job, after the command returns. Auto-queue may overestimate or underestimate a driver's load until then.
