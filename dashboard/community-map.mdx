The **Map** page shows one community's geography in a single view: its bounding box, zones, places, recent ride demand, and live transit. Use it to add or import the places riders see, draw pricing zones, and connect mobility feeds.

The old **Zones** and **Locations** URLs, `/zones` and `/locations`, redirect to this page.

## Prerequisites

- An `admin` or `intern` dashboard role. Interns only see their assigned community and can't edit zones or mobility feeds. See [Dashboard roles and access](/administration/dashboard-roles).
- A community with a bounding box. Without one, the page shows "This community has no bounding box set. Set one to use the map." See [Communities](/dashboard/communities#geography).

## Open the map

1. Open **Map** in the **Community** group of the sidebar.
2. Pick a community in the community selector in the sidebar.

Admins also see this map at the bottom of the **Fleet** page, without the mobility feeds layer.

## Choose map layers

Open the layer panel on the map to turn layers on and off.

| Layer | Default | Who sees it | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Locations** | On | Everyone | Places with **Show on map** turned on. Click a pin to edit the place. |
| **Zones** | On | Everyone | The community's zones. |
| **Bounding box** | On | Everyone | The community's service area. |
| **Ride routes** | On | Everyone | Quoted routes from recent ride requests. |
| **Users** | Off | Admins | Last known user locations. These can be out of date. |
| **Ride destinations** | Off | Admins | Destinations of unpaid **Quote** rides and **Confirmed** rides. |
| **Community content** | Off | Everyone | A preview of the rider app's community map. |
| **Mobility feeds** | Off | Admins | Live vehicles plus transit routes and stops. |

### Ride routes

When **Ride routes** is on, pick a window of **7d**, **14d**, **30d**, or **60d**. Blue lines are a **Requested route**. Red lines are a **Pricing decline**, where a driver declined because of the fare. The panel shows how many routes are on the map. Windows with more than 2,000 routes show only the newest 2,000.

To compare routes with pricing zones while you edit fares, see [Ride pricing](/dashboard/ride-pricing#work-with-pricing-geography).

### Community content

**Community content** shows what riders see on the app's community map:

- **Places**: the community's top locations. See [Community top locations](/guides/community-top-locations).
- **Events**: the community's events. See [Events and The Spot](/dashboard/feed-and-events).
- **Heading to**: anonymized recent trips. The community's activity window controls how far back these go. See [Map settings](#map-settings).
- **Presence**: a heatmap of where community members were last seen. See [Heat cells](#heat-cells).

The panel counts each item type. The preview only loads when you turn the layer on.

### Mobility feeds

**Mobility feeds** shows **Routes**, **Stops**, and **Vehicles** from the community's enabled feeds. The panel counts routes, stops, and vehicles, and the layer refreshes every minute. See [Manage mobility feeds](#manage-mobility-feeds).

## Map settings

Admins see a **Map settings** button on the **Map** page. It opens a dialog with the server-side inputs to what the app's community map shows: **Heat cells** and the **Activity window**. Interns don't see the button.

### Heat cells

The rider app's presence heatmap groups members' last-known positions into square grid cells. Each cell shows how many people are in it. Use these settings to trade detail for coverage: smaller cells show more precise heat but drop more cells that fall below the minimum.

| Setting | Range | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cell size** | 25 m to 2 km | About 50 m | The width of each grid cell. |
| **Minimum people per cell** | 3 to 25 | 3 | Yelo drops cells with fewer people. The floor of 3 keeps a cell from singling out one person's position, so you can raise it but not lower it. |
| **Count people seen in the last** | 1 to 365 days | 60 days | Yelo ignores last-known positions older than this. |
| **Cell limit per community** | 50 to 2,000 | 500 | Yelo keeps only this many cells per community, heaviest first. |

Only members who share their location with friends count toward heat.

#### Preview before you save

As you move the sliders, the dialog previews your draft against live positions for the community selected in the sidebar. Nothing changes in the app until you save.

- The cell count your draft produces, compared with what the app shows now, and the share of people who land in a shown cell.
- A chart of the cell count at every cell size. Click a bar to pick that size. The outlined bar is the size with the most cells at 300 m or under. Select **Use best cell size** to pick it.
- The cells themselves, drawn on a small map.
- A table of every active, visible community with **People**, **Cells now**, **With these settings**, and **Best cell size**.

#### Save for one community or all

Pick where your draft applies, then select **Save heat cell settings**:

- **{Community} only** saves the values as that community's own settings. They replace the platform-wide values for that community.
- **All communities** saves the values for every community without its own settings. It also removes the selected community's own settings, so it follows the platform-wide values again.

Yelo recomputes every community's heat cells as soon as you save. The app shows the new heatmap within a few minutes.

### Activity window

The activity window sets how far back, in hours, anonymized "people heading to…" activity appears on the rider map for the selected community. Enter a whole number from 1 to 720 and select **Save window**. The default is 168 hours, which is 7 days.

## Manage zones

Zones are polygons inside a community. Active zones count for pricing, and visible zones appear on the rider map. For how zones affect fares, see [Pricing](/concepts/pricing).

Only admins can edit zones. Interns see "Zone editing requires administrator access." under the map.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draw the zone">
    Turn on **Zones**, then use the polygon tool on the map to draw the zone's outline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the details">
    Fill in the **New zone** dialog. See the fields below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Select **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The zone name. Required. |
| **Description** | Rider-facing info shown in the zone tooltip. |
| **Color** | The zone's color on the map. |
| **Priority** | A whole number, zero or greater. Higher priority wins where zones overlap. On a tie, the smaller zone wins. |
| **Active (counts for pricing)** | Whether pricing uses the zone. |
| **Show on map (riders see it)** | Whether riders see the zone on the app map. |

The **Zones** list under the map shows each zone with an icon for whether it's visible on the map. Use the edit icon to open the **Edit zone** dialog, or the delete icon to remove the zone.

<Warning>
  Deleting a zone takes effect right away. The dashboard doesn't ask you to confirm, and the zone's pricing overrides are deleted with it.
</Warning>

If an imported operating-hours calendar still references the zone, the delete fails and the page shows **Failed to delete zone**.

## Manage locations

Locations are the named places Yelo knows about in a community, such as a student center or a stadium. Riders see them as pins on the app map when **Show on map** is on. You can also feature them in rider search. See [Community top locations](/guides/community-top-locations).

The **Locations** list under the map shows **Name**, **Address**, **Categories**, **Map**, and **Created**. Places on the rider map show an **On map** badge. The list shows up to 500 locations.

### Add a location

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the form">
    Select **Add Location** above the map.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the details">
    Fill in the fields below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Select **Add Location**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Field | Required | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Name** | Yes | The place name. |
| **Address** | Yes | The street address. |
| **Categories** | No | A comma-separated list, such as `dining, study`. |
| **Latitude** and **Longitude** | No | The place's coordinates. |
| **Description** | No | A short description. |
| **Image URL** | No | A link to an image of the place. |
| **Show on map** | No | Shows the place as a pinned point of interest on the app map. The first category sets its icon. |

To edit a location, click its pin on the map or select the edit icon in the list. To delete it, select the delete icon. Deletion happens right away.

### Import locations and zones

Select **Import** above the map and choose a `.kml` or `.csv` file. When the import finishes, a message summarizes how many locations and zones were added and how many failed. For how imports work under the hood, see [Curated POIs and imports](/engineering/location/poi-catalog-and-imports).

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="CSV">
    A CSV file imports locations only. The first row must be a header row with a `name` column. Yelo also reads these optional columns: `address`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `description`, `image_url`, and `categories`.

    Each row needs a name plus either an address or both coordinates. Yelo skips other rows. It looks up coordinates for rows that only have an address.

    ```csv
    name,address,categories
    Student Center,123 University Ave,"dining, study"
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="KML">
    A KML file, such as a Google My Maps export, can import both locations and zones.

    - Each point placemark becomes a location. The name of its folder becomes the location's category, in singular form. For example, a folder named `Restaurants` becomes the category `restaurant`.
    - Each polygon placemark becomes a zone with priority `0` and a color from a fixed palette. Yelo skips a polygon when the community already has a zone with the same name.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Manage mobility feeds

Admins see a **Mobility feeds** panel below the map. It manages live vehicle feeds and static transit route and stop archives for the community.

| Column | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | **Active** or **Inactive**. Toggle it to turn the feed on or off. |
| **Name** | The feed name. |
| **Source** | **GBFS**, **GTFS Schedule**, or **GTFS Realtime**. |
| **Vehicle type** | The vehicle type for the feed's vehicles. GTFS Schedule feeds show **Derived from routes**. |
| **URL** | The feed URL. |
| **Created** | When the feed was added. |

### Add a feed

1. Select **Add feed**.
2. Enter a **Name** and choose a **Source**.
3. Choose a **Vehicle type**, if the source needs one.
4. Paste the **Feed URL**:
   - **GBFS**: a GBFS auto-discovery URL, or a direct `vehicle_status` or `free_bike_status` URL. Station-only systems without individual vehicle positions aren't supported.
   - **GTFS Schedule**: a direct GTFS ZIP URL, or a Mobility Database GTFS Schedule page. Yelo resolves catalog pages to their latest ZIP archive.
   - **GTFS Realtime**: a URL that returns a GTFS-Realtime VehiclePositions protobuf feed.
5. Select **Add feed**.

The **Relevant Mobility Database feeds** section suggests active public feeds whose service area overlaps the community's bounding box. Suggestions can carry **Official** or **Enabled** badges. Review the catalog entry, then select **Add feed** to add one.

You can edit a feed's name, vehicle type, and URL later. You can't change its source.

<Note>
  Yelo caches GTFS Schedule ZIPs for a day. Disabling or deleting a feed removes its routes and stops from the map on the next refresh.
</Note>
